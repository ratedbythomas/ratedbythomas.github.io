Panda & Sons

Edinburgh, ScotlandVisited April 2026
“You'd be stumbling home after a few of these.”
What I drank   /   01

Solasta

Ingredients

Johnnie Walker Black Label · Lagavulin 16 · Marsala Wine · Pastis · Lemon Verbena

The profile

Copper color. Orange peel garnish.

My thoughts

Quite a complex whisky drink. Using premium Scotch is a good start when that's the bulk of the drink. Tons of peat, oak, vanilla, tobacco, everything you'd assume with a drink of this nature.

Where it gains some depth is with the fortified wine, pastis, and verbena. Adding, in order, toffee, anise, and fresh zest to balance out the drink.

Very spirit-forward, but that's what I'd want and expect in a Scotch cocktail. You'd be stumbling home after a few of these.

“Using a technique created by Panda & Sons in 2018 called 'Switching.'”
What I drank   /   02

Coconut Daiquiri

Ingredients

Blanco Rum · Roasted Coconut · Acids

The profile

Pearl color. White chocolate garnish (I took a bite before the pic, whoops).

My thoughts

This was the bartender's recommendation. Using a technique created by Panda & Sons in 2018 called "Switching," where they freeze a box of the spirit with the lid off, causing the liquid to split into two parts. Frozen water, and a non-frozen liquid of higher ABV.

Then they remove the frozen water and add in a flavored liquid to enhance the palate and bring the spirit back to its original strength.

Now, while I find this technique fascinating, I'm not sure if I really felt the full potential in this drink.

Now, when a bartender recommends me something, I almost always say yes. But maybe in this case I shouldn't have. Neither daiquiris nor coconut are really my thing.

Good-tasting drink, but didn't blow my socks off.

The Space

I'm going to start this by going off on a tangent that isn't a direct shot at Panda & Sons, but at the speakeasy culture in general.

You cannot simply call yourself a speakeasy just because you can't see the bar from the street. Even if there is somewhat of a disguise, like in this case, a "barbershop". But there is no barbershop. That's just on the window. All you do is walk down some steps and you're in the bar.

I'm passionate about this. You're not a real speakeasy unless you are properly hidden/inside another business.

Moving on... I still do like the idea of being a speakeasy, even if I think it's a half-assed job.

Once inside, it is fairly spacious. Plenty of seating for groups, and only a small bar top with maybe 6 seats. Underground, dark, pub vibes of sorts.

All the bartenders were extremely friendly as well.

Extras

The bartenders were kind enough to let me try Buckfast, which is a famous fortified wine in Scotland, albeit from England.

What makes this fortified wine unique is that it is caffeinated. 30 mg per 100 mL. The taste is of dark fruits and is very herbaceous.

Quite enjoyable and a cool little treat to try.

My Verdict

7.8 / 10Atmosphere

Taste reflects what I drank. Atmosphere reflects the bar experience.
